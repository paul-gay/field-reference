> **Your fifteen second intro**
>
> "Every Leica lens is all metal. The black ones are anodized aluminum, so they're light. Some silver and Classic lenses are chrome over brass, so they're heavier and age beautifully. The aspherical elements are shaped and polished one at a time."

## M lens finishes
| Finish | Detail |
|---|---|
| Black anodized aluminum | The color is part of the metal surface, not paint. Light for all day carry. Summicron-M 35 ASPH: ~255g. |
| Silver (check which) | Older silver lenses were chrome over brass and much heavier. The current Summilux-M 50 ASPH and Summicron-M 35 silver are anodized aluminum and weigh the same as black. |
| Chrome on brass (Classic Line) | Summilux-M 50 Classic: ~417g of chrome plated brass. The 35 "Steel Rim" adds a stainless steel front ring. |
| Glossy black paint on brass | Piano black lacquer over brass on the Summilux-M 50 Classic. Wears to brass at the edges. |
| Safari (olive on aluminum) | Matte olive green, chrome brass focus tab and green leather case. Not numbered, made in limited quantities. |

## Inside an M lens
- **Glass:** mostly Schott raw glass. Each aspherical element is shaped and polished, 30 to 60 minutes apiece.
- **APO lenses** add anomalous partial dispersion glass to remove color fringes.
- **6 bit code** on the mount tells a digital M which lens is attached.
- **Hoods:** modern ASPH lenses have built in slide or twist out hoods. The Summicron-M 35 uses a screw on metal hood.
- **Made:** Wetzlar, with hand adjustment of each lens. Since 2020 some US market M lenses have been assembled at Leica Portugal. Check the box before saying where.

## SL lens build
- **Barrels:** full metal. Summicron-SL f/2 primes are black anodized magnesium and aluminum.
- **Sealing:** all SL lenses are dust and splash protected. Only some, like the new Summilux-SL 50, carry a stated IP54 rating.
- **AquaDura:** a front coating that makes water bead and roll off.
- **Where made:** APO-Summicron-SL and the 2026 Summilux-SL 50 in Germany; Summicron-SL f/2 at Leica Portugal. The 24–70 and 28–70 f/2.8 zooms say Made in Japan and are widely reported to share Sigma optics, with Leica's metal barrel, sealing and AquaDura.

## If they say
- **"Isn't the SL 24–70 just a Sigma?"** The optical design is widely reported to be shared. Leica adds an all metal barrel, sealing, AquaDura and its own testing. If price matters most, the Sigma is a fair choice, and it fits the same mount.

## Accuracy traps
- Don't assume silver is heavier. Check the spec.
- Don't claim IP54 for every SL lens.
- Don't say Wetzlar unless the lens says so.
- Say "aspherical," not "hand ground," unless Leica says it.
