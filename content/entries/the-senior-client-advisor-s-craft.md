## Six clients you will meet

Client| What they're really buying| Where to start  
---|---|---  
The collector| History, provenance, limited editions.| Editions, auction results, the Classic Store. Know serial number lore and be humble when they know more than you.  
The returning film shooter| Their younger self, or their father's camera.| M6, MP, M-A, or M11-D for film feeling with digital files.  
The first Leica| Design, status, and "I want to take better photos of my life."| Q3 or Q3 43. Autofocus, one perfect lens, zero intimidation. Upsell accessories, not complexity.  
The Q owner ready for more| Interchangeable lenses and more intention.| M11 or M EV1 with a 35 or 50 Summicron. Plan the lens journey over years.  
The working pro| Reliability, workflow, video.| SL3-P or SL3-S, L-Mount Alliance lenses, service support. Speak specs fluently.  
The luxury gifter| A meaningful object for someone else.| Sofort 2, D-Lux 8, Q3, ZM watches, small leather goods. Ask about the recipient, not the budget first.  
  
## Discovery questions that open the real conversation

  * "What are you photographing most right now, and what's frustrating you about how it turns out?"
  * "Do you want the camera to decide things for you, or do you want to decide?"
  * "Is this a camera you want to carry every day, or one for trips and projects?"
  * "When you imagine the best photo you'll take with it, what's in it?"

## Objections, answered honestly

They say| A strong, honest answer  
---|---  
"Sony or Fuji does more for less."| "You're right on features per dollar. Leica is built to do fewer things with less between you and the picture: the lenses, the controls, the build, and a camera that stays useful and serviceable for decades. Hold both and tell me which one you'd actually carry."  
"Manual focus scares me."| Offer the Q3 first. If they want M lenses, show the M EV1. If they're curious, let them try zone focusing on the M in the store.  
"Isn't this a Panasonic inside?"| Some Leica models share components with partners, and it's fine to say so. What's Leica's own is the design, the lenses, the color science and interface, the German build of flagship bodies, and the service relationship.  
"I'll wait for the M12."| "Leica hasn't announced one, and I'd never tell you to buy around a rumor. What I can tell you is what the M11 does today and how your lenses carry forward to any future M."  
"It's too expensive."| Talk about cost of ownership: value retention, the Pre-Owned and trade in path, financing, and extended warranty. Then stop talking and let them think.  
  
## Clienteling habits

  * Record every client's current kit, their next logical lens, and what they photograph. The M sale is a multi year lens relationship.
  * Use the test drive program, Akademie workshops, gallery openings and firmware releases as reasons to reach out that aren't sales pitches.
  * Carry your watch clients across. A client who buys an Omega for its history is a natural ZM and M conversation, and vice versa.
  * Send a print. A small print of a photo they took on the demo camera is remembered far longer than a follow up email.
