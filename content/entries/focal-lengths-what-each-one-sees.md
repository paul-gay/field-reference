Focal length| Angle of view (approx.)| How it feels| Classic uses  
---|---|---|---  
21mm| about 91 degrees| Dramatic, immersive, exaggerates foreground.| Landscapes, architecture, interiors, crowds.  
24mm| about 84 degrees| Wide but a little more natural.| Interiors, narrow streets, environmental reportage.  
28mm| about 75 degrees| Wide, energetic, puts you in the scene.| Street, travel, documentary. The Q3's focal length.  
35mm| about 63 degrees| Natural storytelling width, not too wide or tight.| Street, everyday life, family, reportage. The most popular M focal length.  
50mm| about 47 degrees| Close to how we see, calm and classic.| Portraits, details, street, still life.  
66mm| between 50 and 75| A slightly tighter normal view, subtle compression.| Portraits and details with a classic look.  
75mm| about 32 degrees| Short telephoto, flattering compression.| Head and shoulders portraits, candids from a distance.  
90mm| about 27 degrees| Tighter, isolates subjects.| Portraits, details, landscapes picking out shapes.  
  
> **Viewfinder note**
>
> Modern M rangefinders show frame lines in pairs: 28 and 90, 35 and 135, 50 and 75. There are no frame lines for 21mm or 24mm, so those lenses need an accessory viewfinder, live view, a Visoflex EVF, or the M EV1. The 66mm has no dedicated frame line either; ask Leica's training materials which lines to use with it.
