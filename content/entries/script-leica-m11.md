> **Catchphrase**
>
> "This is the camera Leica is famous for. You focus it by hand, the way photographers have since 1954, and it takes about a minute to learn."

**Then pause.** Hand it over before any more words. The rangefinder does the selling.

## The walkthrough
| Step | Say |
|---|---|
| Description | A 60 megapixel rangefinder: you focus by lining up two images in the viewfinder. |
| Inspiration | The M3 of 1954 introduced this mount, and every M lens since still fits. |
| Features | You see outside the frame, so you catch people walking into the picture. Choose 60, 36 or 18 megapixels. Lenses from the 1950s still work on it. |
| Style | Machined, quiet, timeless. |
| Other | An M is a system you build over years, one lens at a time. |

## Adapt the opening
- **Q owner:** You already love the Leica look. This is where you choose your own lenses.
- **Film shooter:** Same experience as your film M, with a digital file.
- **Nervous about focus:** Let me set it so you don't have to focus at all. That's zone focusing.

## Put in their hands
Summicron 35, set to f/5.6. "See the bright patch? Turn the ring until the two images become one."

## Skip unless asked
Sensor architecture, processor names, storage details.

## Scene: James, a Q owner thinking about his first M
- **James:** I'm worried I'll miss focus.
- **You:** Everyone is at first. Look through here and turn the ring.
- **James:** (pause) Oh. It just clicked into place.
- **You:** That feeling is why people fall for the M.
