> **Your fifteen second intro**
>
> "These are brand new film cameras, hand assembled in Wetzlar. No firmware, no subscription. The same lenses fit the digital M, so you can shoot film and digital with one set of glass."

## The heritage in five dates
| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1913/14 | Oskar Barnack builds the Ur-Leica to test movie film in a small camera. |
| 1925 | The Leica I goes on sale and starts 35mm photography. |
| 1954 | The M3 debuts at Photokina. The M bayonet is born and hasn't changed. |
| 1984 | The M6 adds a light meter and becomes the most loved film M. |
| 2022 | Leica brings the M6 back into production alongside the MP and M-A. |

## Ask first
- Have you shot film before?
- Do you want a meter, or do you meter by eye?
- Is this your only camera, or next to a digital one?

## Show them
- Fire the M-A with no battery inside.
- Show the M6 meter arrows in the finder.
- Offer to load the first roll together. It removes the fear.

## If they say
- **"Why not buy a used M6?"** Vintage is wonderful and we can talk about it. New means a warranty, a fresh shutter and rangefinder, and a camera whose whole life starts with you.
- **"Film is expensive to shoot."** It slows you down in a good way. Many owners shoot a roll a week, and the same lenses work on a digital M.

## Pair it with
- Summicron-M 35 or 50, or Summaron-M 28 f/5.6 for a tiny classic kit
- Film, strap, half case; Akademie film session

> **Why the rangefinder matters**
>
> The finder shows more than the lens sees, so you watch people walk into the frame and press the shutter at the right moment. Say it in their language: "you see what's about to happen."
