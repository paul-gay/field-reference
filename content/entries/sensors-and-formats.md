Four Thirds| 17.3 x 13| 2| Leica D-Lux 8; Micro Four Thirds systems.  
APS-C| about 23.5 x 15.6| 1.5 (1.6 for Canon)| Many mirrorless cameras; Leica's former CL and TL.  
Full frame (35mm)| 36 x 24| 1| Leica M, Q and SL; the frame Barnack chose in 1913.  
Medium format (digital)| about 44 x 33 or larger| about 0.8| Fujifilm GFX, Hasselblad X; formerly the Leica S.  
Large format (film)| 4 x 5 inches and up| | View cameras for landscape and architecture.  
Concept| Explanation  
---|---  
Crop factor and equivalence| A 35mm lens on APS-C frames like a 52mm on full frame. Depth of field also scales, so a smaller sensor needs a wider aperture for the same look.  
Megapixels| Resolution. More allows bigger prints and cropping, but also bigger files and more visible shake or focus error.  
Pixel size| Larger pixels usually mean better low light and dynamic range at the pixel level.  
BSI (back side illuminated)| Wiring moved behind the light sensitive layer, gathering light more efficiently. The M11 and SL3-P use BSI sensors.  
Stacked sensors| Memory built into the sensor for very fast readout, reducing rolling shutter.  
Color filter array| Most sensors use a Bayer pattern of red, green and blue filters, then interpolate color. Fujifilm uses its own X-Trans pattern. Leica Monochrom sensors have none at all.  
Low pass filter| A slight blur layer that prevents moiré patterns. Many modern high resolution cameras, including Leicas, omit it for sharpness.  
Bit depth| How many tonal steps each pixel records; 14 bit raw files hold far more editing latitude than 8 bit JPEGs.
