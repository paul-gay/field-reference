## Measures a luxury camera store is likely run on

Confirm Leica's exact scorecard with your manager; these are the standard levers in luxury retail and they'll help you ask the right questions.

Measure| What it tells you  
---|---  
Sales versus plan| The headline number, usually tracked by week, month and category.  
Conversion| Of the people who came in with intent, how many bought. Low conversion with high traffic means coaching, not marketing.  
Average transaction value| Whether advisors build complete kits (lens, strap, case, card, protector, warranty) or just ring up bodies.  
Attachment rate| Share of camera sales with a second item. The easiest number to improve with training.  
Clienteling activity| Outreach, appointments booked, and repeat purchase rate from the client book.  
Client experience| Survey scores, reviews, service turnaround, and how complaints are resolved.  
Inventory health| Sell through, weeks of supply, aged stock, demo unit condition.  
  
## Allocation and inventory: where your operations degree pays off

Leica runs deliberately scarce supply. Limited editions and new launches are allocated, and waitlists are a real part of the job. Three habits will set you apart:

  * **A transparent waitlist policy.** Clients forgive waiting. They don't forgive feeling skipped. Know the store's rules and apply them the same way every time.
  * **Demand signals upward.** Track requests you can't fill, by product and configuration, and share that data with your manager. That's demand planning at store level, the same thinking you've built in your coursework.
  * **Demo and pre owned discipline.** Rotate demo units, log their condition, and grade trade ins consistently. A worn demo quietly tells a client the brand doesn't care.

## Leading the team

  * Run a short daily huddle: yesterday's results, today's appointments, one product point, one client story.
  * Build expertise in tiers: every advisor fluent in Q and M basics, with specialists for SL and video, film and collecting, and watches.
  * Role play the objections in Part 6 weekly. Confidence on the floor is rehearsed.
  * Coach in the moment, praise in public, correct in private.

## Opening a new store: the Houston playbook

  * Map the local photography community before opening: camera clubs, working pros, university programs, and museum photography departments. The Museum of Fine Arts, Houston has a respected photography collection, and Houston hosts FotoFest, a long running international photography biennial.
  * Seed the client book early with events (a portrait day, a photo walk, an Akademie workshop) so opening week isn't all walk ins.
  * Build partnerships with local luxury neighbors and hospitality concierges.
  * Set the operating rhythm on day one: opening and closing checklists, cash and stock controls, service intake process, and a weekly business review.
