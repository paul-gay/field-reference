title: "How to use M lenses well"
category: "Lenses"

summary: "Handling details that separate owners from photographers."
---

* **Mounting:** align the red dot on the lens with the red dot on the camera, insert, and turn until it clicks. To remove, press the release button and turn the other way.  
  * **Focus tab:** rest a fingertip on it. Learn that tab at the bottom means roughly a certain distance for your lens; experienced shooters pre focus by feel.
  * **Close focus detent:** on lenses that focus below 0.7m, you'll feel a stop. Beyond it the rangefinder no longer couples, so use live view, a Visoflex, or the M EV1.
  * **External finders:** 21mm and 24mm lenses need an accessory viewfinder on a rangefinder, or live view or an EVF.
  * **Lens protrusion:** some large lenses and hoods block part of the viewfinder. Show clients before they buy.
  * **Hoods and filters:** many modern lenses have built in hoods. Filter sizes vary (39mm, 46mm, 49mm are common), so note them for accessory sales.
  * **Lens caps:** a rangefinder can't see a cap on the lens. Build the habit of checking.
  * **Very fast lenses:** at f/0.95 to f/1.4 the focus zone is razor thin. Rangefinder calibration matters; an EVF or focus peaking helps.
  * **Adapting:** M lenses work on SL cameras with the M to L adapter, and many clients use them on other mirrorless systems too. Coupled rangefinder focusing only exists on M rangefinder bodies.
  * **Old lenses on digital bodies:** some vintage collapsible lenses shouldn't be collapsed on certain digital Ms. Check compatibility first.
