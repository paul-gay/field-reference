**Exposure** is how much light reaches the sensor or film. Too little and the image is dark (underexposed); too much and it's bright and loses highlight detail (overexposed). Three controls set it, and each has a side effect that shapes the picture.

Control| What it changes| Side effect  
---|---|---  
Aperture (f number)| How wide the opening is.| Depth of field: wide apertures blur backgrounds; small apertures keep more sharp.  
Shutter speed| How long light is let in.| Motion: fast speeds freeze action; slow speeds blur movement and risk camera shake.  
ISO| How strongly the signal is amplified.| Noise or grain: higher ISO means more noise and less dynamic range.  
  
## Stops

A **stop** is a doubling or halving of light. Opening from f/8 to f/5.6 is one stop more light. Going from 1/250 to 1/125 is one stop more. ISO 400 to ISO 800 is one stop brighter. Because they all speak in stops, you can trade one for another.

Equivalent exposures (same brightness)| Look of the photo  
---|---  
f/2 at 1/1000, ISO 400| Shallow depth, frozen motion.  
f/5.6 at 1/125, ISO 400| Middle ground.  
f/16 at 1/15, ISO 400| Deep focus, motion blur, possible shake.  
  
## Metering and the histogram

  * **Meters** measure light and suggest an exposure. Modes include matrix or multi field (whole scene), center weighted, and spot (a small area).
  * Meters assume the scene averages to middle gray, so snow looks gray and black cats look gray unless you correct it.
  * **Exposure compensation** tells the camera to go brighter or darker than it suggests, in stops.
  * The **histogram** graphs tones from black (left) to white (right). Data piled against the right edge means clipped highlights.
  * **Exposure value (EV)** is a single number for a combination of settings; each EV step is one stop.

## The sunny 16 rule

In bright sun at f/16, shutter speed equals 1 over ISO. Hazy: open one stop. Overcast: two. Open shade: three. It works on any camera, meter or not.
