## The greeting sequence

  * **Acknowledge within a few seconds.** Eye contact, a smile, a simple welcome, even if you're with someone else. "Welcome in, I'll be right with you" is enough.
  * **Give space.** Let them look for a minute. People need a moment to settle into a new room.
  * **Open with a question that isn't about buying.** Avoid "Can I help you find something?" because the easy answer is "just looking," which ends the conversation.
  * **Get a camera into their hands** as soon as they show interest in one.

## Openers that work

Situation| Try  
---|---  
They're looking around the space| "Welcome in. Have you visited a Leica Store before?" If no: "Then let me give you the thirty second version of who we are."  
They're looking at a specific case| "That's the Q3. It's the camera most people fall for first. Would you like to hold it?"  
They're looking at the gallery wall| "These were all made on Leica cameras. Is there one that caught your eye?"  
They're carrying a camera| "Nice, what are you shooting with?" Every photographer loves this question.  
They say "just looking"| "Perfect, that's what we're here for. Everything in the cases can come out, so just wave me over." Then actually leave them alone.  
  
## Handing over a camera

This is the single most important habit. Put the strap around their wrist or neck first, then place the camera in their hands. Show them where to look and one thing to do: "Look through here, and turn this ring." Then be quiet for a moment and let them experience it. The weight, the machined metal and the sound of the shutter make the case for the brand better than words. Follow your store's security procedure every time, which keeps this generous gesture safe.
