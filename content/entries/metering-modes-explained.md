## What metering does
Metering measures the light in the scene and suggests an exposure. Every meter assumes the scene averages out to **middle gray**, which is why snow comes out gray and a black cat comes out gray unless you correct it.

## The modes
| Mode | What it measures | Use it for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Multi field** (matrix, evaluative) | The whole frame in zones, weighed by the camera's logic. | Most everyday scenes; the safe default. | Strong backlight or a small bright subject on a dark background. |
| **Center weighted** | The whole frame, with extra emphasis on the center. | Portraits with the subject in the middle; predictable results once you know it. | Off center subjects. |
| **Spot** | A small area, usually around the focus point or center. | Backlit faces, stage performers, the moon, a bright subject against darkness. Precise, deliberate control. | Meter the wrong spot and the whole exposure is off. |
| **Highlight weighted** (on some Leicas) | Protects the brightest areas from clipping. | Stage lighting, sunlit faces in shade, high contrast scenes where bright detail matters. | Shadows can go darker; lift them later from raw. |

## Film M metering
The **M6 and MP** use a selective, center area reading shown as LED arrows in the finder: balance the arrows and you're exposed for the middle of the frame. The **M-A has no meter**; use a handheld meter, a phone app, or the sunny 16 rule.

## Tools that work with metering
- **Exposure compensation:** tell the camera to go brighter (+) or darker (−) than it suggests. Snow: around +1 to +2. A black subject: around −1.
- **AE lock (AEL):** meter a point, lock it, then reframe.
- **Histogram:** check exposure objectively. Data piled against the right edge means clipped highlights.
- **Auto ISO with a minimum shutter speed:** lets the camera hold exposure while you control aperture and motion.

## Exposure modes, briefly
| Mode | You set | The camera sets |
|---|---|---|
| **P (program)** | Nothing (or a shifted pair) | Aperture and shutter |
| **A (aperture priority)** | Aperture | Shutter speed. The most common mode on Leicas; on an M, set the shutter dial to A. |
| **S (shutter priority)** | Shutter speed | Aperture |
| **M (manual)** | Both | Nothing; the meter just advises |

>
> Mode names and which modes each Leica offers vary by model. Confirm in the camera's menu.
