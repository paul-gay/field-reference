Because the M mount hasn't changed since 1954, a lens made in the 1950s will mount on an M11. Screw mount lenses fit with a simple adapter. That compatibility is almost unique in photography and is a big reason M owners stay for decades.

Lens family| Why it's legendary  
---|---  
Summicron 50 f/2| The classic standard lens since the 1950s, known for sharpness in a compact size.  
Summicron 35 f/2| Arguably the quintessential street lens. Early versions, like the "8 element" from the late 1950s, are prized by collectors.  
Summilux f/1.4 lenses| Fast lenses introduced from 1959 onward, loved for low light and rendering.  
Noctilux| The night lens: f/1.2 in 1966, f/1.0 in 1976, f/0.95 in 2008, and the Noctilux-M 35 in 2026.  
APO lenses| Modern designs with exceptional color correction, such as the APO-Summicron-M 50.  
Classic Line| Modern reissues and reinterpretations of historic designs, including the Summicron-M 66 f/2 of 2026.  
  
Collectors and photographers often talk about a lens's **rendering** or "look": how it draws out of focus areas, contrast, glow in highlights, and how it handles backlight. Older lenses often have softer, glowier character; modern APO lenses are clinically precise. Neither is better; they're different paintbrushes.

> **Accuracy note**
>
> Some very old collapsible lenses should not be collapsed on certain digital Ms, because they can hit the camera's internals. When clients bring vintage lenses, check compatibility before mounting.
