## The idea
**Dynamic range** is the span from the darkest to the brightest tone a sensor or film can record with detail, measured in stops. Modern full frame sensors hold roughly 12 to 15 stops (the SL3-P is rated up to 14). A sunny street with deep shade can easily exceed that.

## Signs you've exceeded it
- The histogram touches **both edges**.
- Highlight warnings flash in the sky while shadows are pure black.

## What to do
| Approach | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Choose what matters** | Expose for the important part and let the rest go. | Street, documentary, silhouettes. |
| **Protect highlights, lift shadows** | Expose so whites are safe, then brighten shadows from raw. | Most digital work. |
| **Add light** | Fill flash, a reflector, or move the subject. | Portraits in harsh sun or backlight. |
| **Reduce light where it's brightest** | A graduated ND filter darkens the sky. | Landscapes. |
| **Bracket and blend** | Shoot several exposures and combine them (HDR). | Interiors, architecture, still scenes on a tripod. |
| **Wait for better light** | Golden hour, overcast, open shade. | Anything that can wait. |

## Raw gives you room
A raw file holds far more editing latitude than a JPEG, especially in the shadows. Leica cameras record raw as DNG.
