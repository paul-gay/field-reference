### The rangefinder

You look through a window near the corner of the camera, not through the lens. In the center is a small bright patch showing a second image of the scene. As you turn the lens's focus ring, the two images move. When they line up into one, that part of the scene is in focus.

### Frame lines

Bright lines inside the viewfinder show what each lens will capture. Because the window is wider than the frame, you can see what's about to enter the picture. Photographers love this for street work.

### Zone focusing

Instead of focusing each shot, you set the lens to a distance range (for example, everything from about 2 to 5 meters sharp at f/8) and just shoot. It's how many street photographers work quickly with a manual focus camera, and it's a great way to reassure someone nervous about manual focus.

### Why the lenses are so small

A rangefinder has no mirror box, so lenses can sit close to the sensor. That's why M lenses are famously compact for their quality.
