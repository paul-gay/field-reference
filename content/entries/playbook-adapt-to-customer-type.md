Different people are drawn to different parts of the story. Figure out which kind of visitor you have, then lead with what brought them.

| Visitor | Approach | Leica version |
|---|---|---|
| **Knows nothing about the brand** | Show real excitement to introduce it, learn their interests, and lead with the values that will appeal most. | "You're new to Leica? I'm glad I get to tell you about it." Then the 20 second story. |
| **Interested in one product** | Connect that product to the values and history behind it. | Someone at the Q3 case: connect it to the Leica idea of the camera you always carry. |
| **Interested in an ambassador or artist** | Link the person they admire to the brand and its values. | A client who loves a Leica photographer: talk about that work and the camera behind it. |
| **Came via a friend, an ad or a partnership** | Find out what attracted them, or why the friend recommended the brand, and connect it to the values. | "What did your friend love about theirs?" |
| **Returning owner** | They have a personal history with the brand. Update them on how it has evolved, which means knowing the history well. | An M6 owner from decades ago: what's new, and what's exactly the same. |

## The principle
The opening story should come from the client's reason for being there, not from your favorite talking points.
