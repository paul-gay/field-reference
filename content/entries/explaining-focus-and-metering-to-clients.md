## Lines that make it simple
- **Autofocus:** "The camera has to decide which way to move the lens and how far. Newer systems answer both at once, which is why they feel instant."
- **Subject detection:** "It recognizes eyes, faces and even animals, and stays on them as they move. You just frame."
- **Single vs continuous:** "Single is for things that stay still. Continuous is for things that move. Intelligent picks for you."
- **Metering:** "The camera reads the light and assumes everything averages to gray. Most of the time that's right. When the scene is very bright or very dark, you nudge it."
- **Manual focus on the M:** "The M doesn't guess. You decide, and the rangefinder or the EVF shows you exactly when it's sharp."

## Quick demos
- **Q3 or SL3-P:** turn on eye detection and have a colleague walk toward the camera. Show the box staying on the eye.
- **Backlight:** point at a person standing in front of the store window. Shoot in multi field, then spot meter on the face. Show both.
- **M EV1:** turn the focus ring and watch peaking appear.

## Honest answers
| They say | Say something like |
|---|---|
| "Sony focuses faster." | "Sony's autofocus is excellent. The SL3-P is Leica's best yet. What you gain here is the build, the color, the lenses and the bridge to M." |
| "Why doesn't the M have autofocus?" | "It's deliberate: small, quiet, and fully in your control. If you want autofocus with the Leica look, the Q3 or SL may be your camera." |
| "Do I need to understand metering modes?" | "Not to start. Multi field handles most scenes. Learn spot metering when you meet your first backlit portrait." |
| "Does the D-Lux 8 track moving subjects?" | "It uses contrast autofocus, which is accurate but best with subjects that aren't moving fast. For kids and sports, the Q3 or SL3-P." |
