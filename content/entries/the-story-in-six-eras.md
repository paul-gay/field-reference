## Era 1 · The optical workshop (1849 to 1913)

The company's roots are older than the camera. Carl Kellner founded an Optical Institute in Wetzlar in 1849. Ernst Leitz I, who had trained in watchmaking in Neuchâtel, joined the business and became its sole proprietor in 1869, renaming it E. Leitz, Wetzlar. For decades Leitz was a microscope company, and a very good one. That's why the brand's instinct is optical first and camera second.

> **Floor bridge**
>
> The watchmaking detail is your personal bridge. You came from Breitling, Omega and TAG Heuer, and the founder of the company you're joining trained as a watchmaker. Leica's ZM watches aren't a licensing stretch; they're a return to the founder's first trade.

## Era 2 · Barnack's idea (1913 to 1925)

Oskar Barnack, a Leitz development engineer and keen hiker, wanted a camera he could actually carry. Plate cameras of the day were heavy. Around 1913 to 1914 he built the Ur-Leica, a compact camera that used 35mm motion picture film and doubled the cine frame to 24 x 36mm. That frame is still "full frame" today. The First World War stalled the project. In 1924, during an economic crisis, Ernst Leitz II decided to put the camera into production anyway; the line attributed to him is "I hereby decide: It will be risked." Max Berek designed the lenses. The Leica I debuted at the Leipzig Spring Fair in 1925, and the name comes from **LEI** tz **CA** mera.

## Era 3 · Screw mount and the photojournalism revolution (1925 to 1953)

The Leica II of 1932 added a rangefinder coupled to the lens, and the III series refined it. Photojournalism as we know it grew up with these cameras, because a photographer could now work unnoticed in the street. Barnack died in 1936. This era also holds the company's most important moral story, the Leica Freedom Train (see Part 2), and the arrest of Ernst Leitz II's daughter, Elsie Kühn-Leitz, in 1943 for helping a Jewish woman from Wetzlar.

## Era 4 · The M is born (1954 to the 1990s)

At Photokina in 1954, Leica introduced the M3: a quick bayonet mount replacing the screw thread, and a bright combined viewfinder and rangefinder. It's the ancestor of every M camera sold today, and the M mount is still in use more than 70 years later. The line continued through the M2, M4, M5 and the M6 of 1984, the camera most film shooters still dream about. Leica also built SLRs (the Leicaflex of 1964 and the R system), but Japanese SLR makers dominated that market and the rangefinder became a niche. Through the late 1980s and early 1990s the Leitz businesses merged, reorganized and split into separate companies. In 1996 the camera business became Leica Camera AG.

## Era 5 · Crisis, rescue and the digital M (2005 to 2021)

Leica was late to digital and nearly didn't survive it. In 2005 the Austrian entrepreneur Andreas Kaufmann, through his investment company ACM, bought a controlling stake at a time when the company was reportedly close to collapse. What followed is the turnaround story you should be able to tell in a minute:

2006 · M8, the first digital M.

2009 · M9, the first full frame digital rangefinder.

2011 · Blackstone buys a minority stake, still 45% today.

2012 · M Monochrom, a digital camera that can only shoot black and white.

2014 · Headquarters returns to Wetzlar at the new Leitz Park, marking 100 years of the Ur-Leica.

2015 · The Q (fixed 28mm Summilux) and the SL mirrorless system both launch.

2016 · Smartphone partnership begins with Huawei; Xiaomi follows in 2022.

2018 · L-Mount Alliance founded with Panasonic and Sigma.

## Era 6 · Record growth and the centenary (2022 to today)

2022 · M11 with a 60MP sensor and Triple Resolution. Barnack's own 0 Series No. 105 sells for €14.4 million, the most expensive camera ever sold.

2023 · M11-P, the first camera with Content Credentials built in. Q3 launches.

2024 · SL3, M11-D (no rear screen), Q3 43, and the M Edition 70 for 70 years of the M.

2025 · "Century," 100 years of the Leica I. SL3-S. The 0 Series No. 112 sells for €7.2 million. M EV1 arrives in October.

2026 · Noctilux-M 35 (January), Leica Leitzphone powered by Xiaomi (February), Andreas Voll becomes CEO (April), SL3-P (June), Summicron-M 66 f/2 (September), 50 years of Leica Galleries.
