## 1\. A lineage of designers  
  
Designer| Why the name matters  
---|---  
Max Berek| Designed the first Leica lens, a 50mm f/3.5 for Barnack's Ur-Leica of 1913. First called Anastigmat, then renamed Elmax. His Elmar became the standard Leica lens for decades.  
Walter Mandler| A legendary designer, largely at Ernst Leitz Canada in Midland, Ontario. Collectors use "Mandler design" as a mark of quality for many classic lenses, including the long running Summicron 50.  
Peter Karbe| Longtime head of Leica optical design in Wetzlar. Designed the Noctilux-M 50 f/0.95 ASPH. and the modern APO era of M lenses.  
  
## 2\. Correction taken to extremes

**APO** designs use special low dispersion glass so red, green and blue light all focus at the same point, which removes color fringing and raises sharpness. **Aspherical** elements, which Leica calls "extremely elaborate to produce," allow strong correction into the corners without making lenses bigger. Leica was an early pioneer: the first Noctilux of 1966 used aspherical surfaces, and the Summilux 35 ASPHERICAL of 1989 is a collector's piece today. In 2026 the Noctilux-M 35 used precision glass moulding, pressing glass at high temperature, a technique Leica has mastered in house.

## 3\. Small by design

Because a rangefinder has no mirror, M lenses sit close to the sensor and can be remarkably compact. A Summicron-M 35 f/2 weighs about 255g. Small lenses keep the whole camera discreet, which is the point of an M.

## 4\. Mechanical quality you can feel

  * All metal barrels and precisely damped focusing; many lenses use brass focusing helicoids.
  * Focus tabs that let you focus by feel.
  * Crisp aperture clicks, engraved and paint filled scales.
  * Built in, sliding or screw in hoods on many modern designs.

## 5\. Rendering

Photographers talk about Leica "micro contrast," a three dimensional look, smooth transitions from sharp to soft, and graceful handling of backlight. These are partly subjective, which is exactly why the in store comparison matters. Leica also offers deliberate choices of character: clinical APO lenses on one side, and Classic Line lenses that recreate vintage glow on the other.

## 6\. Compatibility across seventy years

The M bayonet hasn't changed since 1954, so lenses and bodies from across the decades work together. Modern lenses carry a 6 bit code that lets digital Ms identify the lens, apply corrections and record it in the file. M lenses also adapt to SL cameras and to many other mirrorless systems.

## 7\. Longevity and value

A well cared for Leica lens can be serviced and used for decades, and there is a deep secondhand market through Leica Pre-Owned, the Classic Store and the Leitz Photographica Auction.
