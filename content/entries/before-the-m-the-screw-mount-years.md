The Leica I (1925) had a fixed lens and no rangefinder; photographers guessed distance or used a separate accessory rangefinder. The Leica II of 1932 coupled a rangefinder to the lens, so turning the focus ring moved the rangefinder image. Lenses screwed into the body on a 39mm thread, which photographers now call LTM (Leica Thread Mount). The III series added features like slow shutter speeds, and the IIIg of 1957 was the last of the screw mount line.

## What photographers wanted fixed

  * **Separate windows.** On the screw mount cameras you framed through one small window and focused through another.
  * **Slow lens changes.** Screwing lenses in and out took time, the moment could pass while you did it.
  * **Dim, small viewfinders.** Framing was cramped and not very bright.
  * **Knob film advance.** Winding film with a knob was slower than a lever.

> **Why it's called M**
>
> M stands for _Messsucher_ , German for rangefinder. The name describes the camera's essential idea: a viewfinder that also measures distance.
