## What the meter is telling you
Every reflected light meter, including the one in every camera, suggests the exposure that would make what it's measuring look **middle gray**. Most scenes average out close to that. Very bright or very dark scenes fool it.

## Reading it on Leica cameras
| Camera | What you see | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Digital in A mode** | The camera picks the shutter speed for your aperture. | Override with exposure compensation for bright or dark scenes; confirm with the histogram. |
| **Digital in M mode** | A scale from −3 to +3 with a marker. | Adjust aperture or shutter until the marker sits at 0, or deliberately off 0. |
| **M6 and MP** | Two triangle LEDs and a center dot in the viewfinder. | Turn the aperture ring or shutter dial the way the lit arrow points until only the dot glows. Check the manual for your model. |
| **M-A** | No meter. | Use a handheld meter, a phone app, or sunny 16. |

## Reflected vs incident
- **Reflected** (in camera): light bouncing off the subject. Fast, but fooled by very bright or dark subjects.
- **Incident** (handheld meter with a white dome): stand where the subject is and point the dome at the camera. It measures the light falling on the scene, so white and black subjects both come out correct.

## When to override
| Scene | The meter will… | So you… |
|---|---|---|
| Snow, beach, white wall | Make it gray | Add +1 to +2 |
| Black subject, dark suit, night street | Make it washed out | Subtract about −1 |
| Backlit face | Darken the face | Spot meter the face, or add +1 to +2 |
| Stage performer in a spotlight | Overexpose them | Spot meter the performer, or subtract |

## Tricks
- **Meter a stand in:** gray pavement, green grass or blue northern sky are close to middle gray. Meter, lock, recompose.
- **Your palm:** in the same light, a palm usually reads about one stop brighter than middle gray, so open up about one stop from its reading. Test it with your own hand first.
- **Sunny 16 sanity check:** in bright sun at f/16, shutter speed is about 1 over ISO.
