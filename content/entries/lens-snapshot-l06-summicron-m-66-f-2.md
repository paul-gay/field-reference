## The Story
**A secret commission.** In the late 1960s, Dr Walter Mandler at Ernst Leitz Canada designed the Elcan 66mm f/2 for the US Navy, for covert reconnaissance. Only about 200 were made.

**Why 66mm.** It wasn't chosen for marketing. It came out of Mandler's calculation balancing resolution, f/2 speed and a compact size.

**September 2026.** Leica reinterpreted it for the Classic Line: 660 lenses worldwide, each with its own three digit number and a certificate.

## The Craft
### Outer strengths
- Historic style engravings, including a Leitz Wetzlar inscription.
- A distance scale laid out to the original military specification.
- Screw on hood; about 456g.

### Inner strengths
- **Six elements in five groups**, based on the original design.
- High contrast with soft bokeh wide open; maximum resolution stopped down.
- **9 aperture blades**, E49 filter thread, 6 bit coding.

## In Hand
### See
- The engraved "60" on the lens: it means 66.0mm, marked to a tenth of a millimeter.
- A natural view between 50 and 75mm.

### Touch
- The aperture ring turns the opposite way from other M lenses, just like the original. Point this out before they shoot.

### Feel
- Holding a once classified piece of Leica history.
- Subtle compression that flatters portraits.
