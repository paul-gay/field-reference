## The structure
Before presenting anything, you've welcomed the person and discovered what they care about. Then:

1. **Description.** The product in one sentence.
2. **Inspiration.** The original piece or story behind it, and any partner it was made with.
3. **Features.** What the client can't see, told as what it does for their daily life.
4. **Style.** Adjectives that match both the brand's voice and the client's own style.
5. **Other.** Anything specific that will interest this person.

## The catchphrase
The catchphrase is simply **description plus inspiration**, said in one short, simple sentence. It shows the client you understood what they need.

- It doesn't need to be personalized. Choosing the right model for them already did that.
- The **arguments** that follow should be personalized.
- Build it from a few keywords that capture the model's personality, and keep the language inclusive so anyone can picture themselves with it.

## Then stop and listen
After the catchphrase, pause and let the client react. If the reaction is positive, continue: put the piece in their hands (or on their wrist) and move to features.

## How it transfers to Leica
| Step | Q3 43 example |
|---|---|
| Catchphrase | "I think you'll love this one: a full frame Leica that sees the way your eye does." |
| Description | "A full frame Leica with a 43mm APO lens, made for people and everyday life." |
| Inspiration | The idea since Barnack: a serious camera small enough to always have with you. |
| Features | For a parent: autofocus and a natural perspective for family portraits. For a detail lover: APO sharpness. |
| Style | Understated, precise, quietly confident. |
| Other | A workshop or photo walk they could join. |
