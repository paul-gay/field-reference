## The reasons they give

Reason| What it means in practice  
---|---  
You see outside the frame| The rangefinder window is wider than the picture. You watch someone walk toward the frame and press the shutter at the right moment. No other camera type does this.  
Discretion| Small, quiet, unthreatening. People react less to an M than to a big camera, so pictures are more natural.  
Intention| Manual focus and simple controls make you decide. Many photographers say it makes them see more carefully.  
Tactile pleasure| Machined metal, precise focus rings, clicking aperture rings. It's a joy to handle, like a fine mechanical watch.  
Longevity| Lenses from the 1950s still work, film bodies can be serviced for decades, and the system holds value well.  
The lenses| Small, sharp, with character. Many people choose the M for the glass first.  
Anticipation| Especially with film or the M11-D: you don't check the screen, you stay in the moment.  
  
## The honest limitations

  * No autofocus. It takes practice, and fast moving subjects are harder.
  * Close focus is typically around 0.7 meters with many lenses, though some newer lenses focus closer. Macro and very close portraits are limited without live view or an EVF.
  * Long lenses (above 90mm) are harder to focus and frame accurately through a rangefinder.
  * The rangefinder can't see a lens cap or a finger over the lens on film. Digital Ms show it in playback or live view.
  * The rangefinder needs occasional calibration, especially after a knock.
  * It's expensive.

> **Why saying this helps**
>
> Clients trust an advisor who names the limits. It also helps you route people to the M EV1, the Q or the SL when that's the better fit, and those clients come back.
