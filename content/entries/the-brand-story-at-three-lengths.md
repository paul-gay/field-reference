## The 20 second version

> **Say it like this**
>
> "Leica is the company that invented the small 35mm camera, a hundred years ago in Wetzlar, Germany. Before that, serious cameras were big and heavy. Leica made it possible to carry a camera everywhere, and that's how street photography and modern photojournalism were born. We still build cameras by hand in Wetzlar today."

## The two minute version

> **Say it like this**
>
> "Leica started as an optics company. Leitz made microscopes in Wetzlar from the 1800s. One of their engineers, Oskar Barnack, loved hiking and hated hauling heavy plate cameras, so around 1914 he built a tiny prototype that used movie film. The frame size he chose is what we still call full frame.
>
> In 1924, in the middle of an economic crisis, the owner, Ernst Leitz II, decided to take the risk and produce it. It launched in 1925, and it changed photography. Photographers like Henri Cartier-Bresson could suddenly work in the street without being noticed.
>
> Today there are three main families. The M, which you focus by hand through a rangefinder, is the heart of the brand. The Q is a full frame camera with a single brilliant lens and autofocus, and it's where most people start. And the SL is our professional system for photo and video. They're still assembled in Wetzlar; the factory only makes around 45 M cameras a day."

## The ten minute conversation

Don't monologue for ten minutes. The long version is the two minute story plus whatever threads the person pulls on. Use Part 5 to follow their interest, and alternate talking and doing:

  * Two minute story, then a camera in hand.
  * One story matched to what they react to.
  * One demo from Part 6.
  * A question about them: "What would you photograph with this?"
  * An invitation (Part 9).
