## Why the approach is different here

Most people who walk into a Leica Store aren't buying today. Some are curious, some are dreaming, some are researching a purchase that's a year away, and some just liked the window. Leica is a considered purchase, and the brand is built on photographic culture as much as on product. Galleries, workshops, the Akademie and the collector world are all part of what Leica sells. Your job is to make the brand real for whoever is in front of you.

That changes what winning looks like. With a watch client at L&B, the visit often had a single goal. Here, a great visit might end with a stranger holding an M for the first time, a sign up for a photo walk, or a teenager who'll remember the store in ten years when they can afford a Q.

## Three principles

  * **Hospitality before product.** Greet like a host welcoming someone into their home. Product comes when they're ready.
  * **Show, then tell.** A camera in the hand beats any description. Get the object into their hands early.
  * **Less, but better.** Leica's philosophy is _Das Wesentliche_ , "the essential." Your presentation should follow it: one good story, one good demo, then listen. Don't recite the catalog.

> **Your advantage**
>
> You've presented Breitling, Omega, TAG Heuer and David Yurman, and you worked on the Louis Vuitton client care team. You already know how to make an expensive object feel approachable. The only new skill is photography fluency, and that comes fast with a camera in your own hands.
