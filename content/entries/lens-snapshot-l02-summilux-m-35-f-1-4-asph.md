## The Story
**The fast 35.** Leica's Summilux 35 has been a low light favorite of street and documentary photographers for decades. Collectors know early versions by nicknames like Steel Rim.

**Character.** Wide open it's soft and atmospheric; from about f/4 it turns crisp. Two personalities in one lens.

**The latest version.** It focuses as close as 0.4m, with a felt stop at 0.7m where rangefinder coupling ends.

## The Craft
### Outer strengths
- Compact for an f/1.4 lens.
- Focus tab and a clear depth of field scale.
- A detent at 0.7m you can feel.

### Inner strengths
- **f/1.4**: roughly twice the light of an f/2 lens.
- **Aspherical design** for sharpness when stopped down.
- **Close focus to 0.4m** with live view or an EVF.

## In Hand
### See
- The glow of highlights wide open.
- Crisp detail at f/5.6 on the same subject.

### Touch
- The 0.7m detent as you turn past it.
- The focus tab and aperture ring.

### Feel
- Freedom to keep shooting as the light fades.
- One lens that covers a whole day.

## In one frame
If you could own only one M lens, this is the argument: a 35 that works from morning light to candlelight.
