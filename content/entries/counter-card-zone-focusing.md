> **The idea in plain words**
>
> "What's in focus is like a slice of bread cut through the scene. Inside the slice is sharp; in front or behind is soft. The focus ring decides where the slice sits. The aperture decides how thick it is. Zone focusing means you set the slice in advance, then stop thinking about focus and just watch for the moment."  
  
### The settings (35mm lens)

  * **Aperture:** f/8 (f/11 for more margin)
  * **Focus:** about 3m / 10 ft
  * **Sharp zone:** roughly 2 to 7m / 6 to 23 ft
  * **Shutter:** 1/250 or faster
  * **ISO:** Auto

### Distance by eye

  * **6 ft:** a person lying down, or conversation distance
  * **10 ft:** across a small living room, about four steps
  * **15 ft:** the length of a car

### The 60 second demo

  * Preset an M with a 35mm lens: f/8, 10 ft, fast shutter.
  * "Everything from about six feet to over twenty feet will be sharp. You don't need to focus."
  * Client points toward the door and shoots five or six frames.
  * Show the frames at full magnification.
  * "The great street photographers set the camera once, then paid attention to people instead of the camera."

Common mistake| Fix  
---|---  
Aperture left wide open (f/2)| The zone becomes tiny. Check it's f/8 or smaller.  
Low light| Raise ISO, or accept a thinner zone.  
Subject too close| Anything nearer than the zone's front edge will be soft.  
Long lens| Works best at 28 and 35mm; thinner at 50mm; barely practical at 90mm.  
  
> **If they ask "why not autofocus?"**
>
> "Autofocus is great, and the Q3 has excellent autofocus. Zone focusing wins when things happen suddenly and close to you, like on a busy street. There's zero delay because focus is already done, and you're looking at the world instead of a focus point."
