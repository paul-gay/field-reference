Year| Camera| What it introduced or changed  
---|---|---  
1954| M3| The M bayonet, combined bright finder, automatic frame lines, 0.91x finder. The original.  
1958| M2| A simpler, more affordable M with a 0.72x finder and 35mm frame lines, which suited the wide lenses street photographers loved.  
1959| M1| A version without a rangefinder, meant for scientific and technical use. The M EV1 of 2025 revived the idea of an M without a rangefinder.  
1967| M4| Angled rewind crank and faster film loading. A favorite of many photojournalists.  
1971| M5| Through the lens light metering, in a larger body. Innovative but controversial with traditionalists, and it sold modestly.  
1973| CL| A compact M mount camera developed with Minolta.  
Late 1970s to early 1980s| M4-2 and M4-P| Production partly in Canada; the M4-P added 28mm and 75mm frame lines.  
1984| M6| TTL metering with simple LED arrows in the classic M body. Made until 1998, then as the M6 TTL until 2002. Reissued in 2022.  
2002| M7| Aperture priority automatic exposure with an electronically controlled shutter.  
2003| MP| A modern mechanical M with a meter, built for professionals. Still made.  
2014| M-A| Fully mechanical, with no meter and no battery. Still made.  
  
Three film Ms are in production today: the M6, the MP and the M-A. Leica is one of very few companies still building new film cameras.
