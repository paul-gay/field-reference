Focal length (full frame)| Category| Feel and uses  
---|---|---  
14 to 24mm| Ultra wide| Dramatic space; architecture, interiors, landscapes.  
28 to 35mm| Wide| Storytelling, street, travel.  
40 to 58mm| Normal| Close to human perception; everyday, portraits, details.  
75 to 135mm| Short telephoto| Portraits, compression, candids.  
200mm and up| Telephoto| Sports, wildlife, distant subjects.  
Concept| Explanation  
---|---  
Prime versus zoom| Primes have one focal length, and are usually smaller, faster and sharper. Zooms trade some of that for flexibility.  
Fast lens| A wide maximum aperture, like f/1.4. Good in low light and for shallow depth.  
Perspective| Determined by camera to subject distance, not focal length. Telephotos seem to compress space because you stand farther back.  
Elements and groups| Individual glass pieces and how they're cemented together.  
Special glass| Low dispersion (often labeled ED) and apochromatic (APO) designs reduce color fringing.  
Aspherical elements| Non spherical surfaces that correct aberrations in compact designs.  
Coatings| Thin layers that reduce reflections, flare and ghosting, and improve contrast.  
Aberrations| Distortion (bent straight lines), vignetting (dark corners), chromatic aberration (color fringes), coma (smeared points of light), field curvature.  
Bokeh| The quality of out of focus areas: smooth, busy, swirly, or with onion ring patterns in highlights.  
Focus breathing| Framing changes slightly as focus changes; important for video.  
Macro and magnification| 1:1 (life size) means the subject is the same size on the sensor as in real life.  
Tilt shift| Lenses that move relative to the sensor to control perspective or the plane of focus; used in architecture.  
Mount and flange distance| The mount's shape and its distance to the sensor. Short mirrorless flange distances allow adapting many older lenses, including M lenses.
