Principle| Explanation  
---|---  
Rule of thirds| Placing key elements along imaginary lines dividing the frame into thirds. A guideline, not a law.  
Leading lines| Roads, rails and shadows that guide the eye into the picture.  
Framing| Doorways, windows or branches that surround the subject.  
Figure and ground| A subject that stands out clearly from its background.  
Negative space| Empty areas that give the subject room and mood.  
Layers| Foreground, middle ground and background each holding interest.  
Balance and symmetry| Visual weight distributed evenly, or deliberately not.  
Color relationships| Complementary colors (blue and orange) create tension; analogous colors create harmony.  
Gesture and timing| The decisive moment: when form and meaning align, in Cartier-Bresson's phrase.  
Point of view| Changing height or distance changes perspective more than changing lenses.  
Seeing in black and white| Without color, you compose with tone, texture, shape and contrast.
