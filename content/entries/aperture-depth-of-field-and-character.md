**Aperture** is the opening in the lens. A wide aperture (small f number, like f/1.4) lets in more light and makes a shallow zone of sharpness, blurring the background. A small aperture (big f number, like f/8) makes more of the scene sharp.

Aperture| Effect| Use it for  
---|---|---  
f/0.95 to f/1.4| Very shallow focus, soft backgrounds, lots of light.| Portraits, night, restaurants, isolating a subject.  
f/2 to f/2.8| Separation with a bit more margin for focus error.| Everyday portraits, indoor life.  
f/4 to f/5.6| Balanced, most lenses are very sharp here.| Travel, groups, general shooting.  
f/8 to f/11| Deep focus, ideal for zone focusing.| Street, landscapes, architecture.  
  
## Character changes with aperture

Many Leica lenses have two personalities. The Classic Line Summilux-M 50 f/1.4, for example, uses a traditional seven element design: wide open it glows with softer contrast and dreamy transitions, and stopped down to f/5.6 or f/8 it becomes much sharper. The APO-Summicron-M 50 is precise and clinical even at f/2. Neither is better; the client's taste decides.

## Bokeh

Bokeh is the quality of the out of focus areas. Clients describe it as creamy, busy, swirly or smooth. Fast lenses and longer focal lengths make it more visible.
