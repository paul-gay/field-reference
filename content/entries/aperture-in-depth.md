The **f number** is focal length divided by the diameter of the opening. That's why a smaller number means a bigger opening, and why f/2 on a 50mm and f/2 on a 90mm give the same brightness.

**Full stop sequence:** f/1, 1.4, 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16, 22. Each step halves the light. Many lenses click in half stops between these.

Concept| Explanation  
---|---  
Depth of field| The zone that looks acceptably sharp. Deeper at small apertures, wider focal lengths and greater distances.  
Sweet spot| Most lenses are sharpest about two to three stops down from wide open, often f/5.6 to f/8.  
Diffraction| At very small apertures (often beyond f/11 to f/16 on high resolution sensors), light bends around the blades and softens the whole image.  
Aperture blades| Their number and shape affect how out of focus highlights look; rounded blades give rounder bokeh.  
T stops| Cinema lenses use T stops, which measure actual light transmission rather than the geometric f number.
