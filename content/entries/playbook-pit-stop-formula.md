## The structure
| Column | Purpose | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| **Storytelling** | Stories behind the collection or piece | Origin year and founder's vision, the name's origin, a cultural moment, where it stands today |
| **Mechanics and know how** | The expert speech | **Outer strengths** (design codes you can recognize at a glance) and **inner strengths** (movement, materials, craft) |
| **Look and feel** | The demonstration | What the client should **see**, **touch** and **feel** |
| **In a nutshell** | The summary | Four or five sentences you could say from memory |

## Why it works
- It follows the order of a real conversation: story first, then substance, then the product in their hands.
- It separates what you *say* (storytelling and expertise) from what you *show* (the demo).
- One page means you can actually learn it.

## How it transfers to Leica
A Leica Q3 on one page:
- **Storytelling:** Barnack's idea of the camera you always carry; the first Q in 2015; today a favorite first Leica.
- **Outer strengths:** the minimalist top plate, the lens with its macro ring, the red dot.
- **Inner strengths:** 60MP full frame sensor, the Summilux 28, hybrid autofocus.
- **See, touch, feel:** see the crop frames; touch the macro ring's click; feel how light it is in one hand.
- **In a nutshell:** "A full frame Leica you can carry every day, with one extraordinary lens and nothing between you and the picture."

This is the same framework I adapted for the Leica watch training materials.
