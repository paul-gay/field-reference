## How to read 8x42
8x is magnification; 42 is the front lens in mm. Exit pupil is 42 divided by 8, about 5.3mm: bigger means brighter at dusk. Twilight factor is the square root of magnification times objective: higher means more detail in low light.

| Spec | Exit pupil | Twilight | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7x42 | 6.0mm | 17.1 | Boats, steadiest view |
| 8x42 | 5.3mm | 18.3 | Birding, all round |
| 10x42 | 4.2mm | 20.5 | Open country, more reach |
| 8x25 | 3.1mm | 14.1 | Travel, opera, stadium |

## Ask, show, answer
- **Ask:** What will you be looking at? Daylight, or dawn and dusk? Glasses? Pocket, bag or neck?
- **Show:** take them to the window; set eyecups and diopter first; compare 8x and 10x on the same target.
- **"Why not cheaper binoculars?"** Look at the edges and the color at dusk. That's where the price shows.
- **"I'm not a birder."** The Noctivid Compact and Monovid go to the opera, the stadium and the museum.

**Know this:** Trinovid HD from an authorized North American dealer carries a 10 year Passport Protection Plan and a 30 year warranty; confirm terms for other models. Hand detailed hunting questions to a trained colleague.
