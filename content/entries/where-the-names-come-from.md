Name| Origin| What it signals today  
---|---|---  
Leica| LEItz CAmera.| The brand itself.  
Elmax| Ernst Leitz plus Max Berek. The renamed first Leica lens, a 50 f/3.5.| Historic only.  
Elmar| Replaced the Elmax in 1925 and derives from it.| Slower lenses, roughly f/3.4 to f/4. Also Super-Elmar, Macro-Elmar, Tele-Elmar.  
Elmarit| A derivation of Elmar.| f/2.8.  
Summar| A 50 f/2 of 1933. Often traced to Latin _summa_ , "highest," though sources differ.| The root of the whole "Summ" family.  
Summicron| Leica says: "summit" plus a contraction of "chroma," color. The first batch was spelled Summikron, alluding to the German _Krone_ , crown. Some enthusiasts link it to crown glass.| f/2.  
Summilux| The Summ prefix plus _lux_ , light.| f/1.4 (f/1.7 on the Q3's 28).  
Noctilux| Latin _nox_ (night) plus _lux_ (light). First a 50 f/1.2 (1966), then f/1.0, then f/0.95.| f/0.95 to f/1.25, the fastest M lenses.  
Summarit| Another derivation of Summar.| Historically f/2.4 to f/2.5 in modern M lenses.  
Summaron| A Summar derivative used on classic wide angles.| Today the tiny Summaron-M 28 f/5.6.  
Hektor| Named after Max Berek's dog.| Historic lenses, usually f/2.5. A great story for collectors.  
Thambar| From the Greek for blurred; a soft focus portrait lens.| Historic, reissued as a modern M lens in 2017.  
ELCAN| Ernst Leitz Canada, Midland, Ontario. Lenses made largely for military use in the 1960s and early 1970s.| Collector territory, and the background story for the new Summicron-M 66.  
  
## Prefixes and suffixes

Label| Meaning  
---|---  
APO| Apochromatically corrected: all colors focus at the same point.  
ASPH.| At least one aspherical element. Leica always writes it with a full stop.  
-M, -SL, -R, -S| The mount: M rangefinder, SL mirrorless, historic R SLR, S medium format.  
Super| Usually a wide angle (Super-Elmar).  
Tele| A telephoto design (Tele-Elmar).  
Macro| Designed for close up work (Macro-Elmar).  
Vario| A zoom lens (Vario-Elmarit-SL).  
Tri| Three focal lengths in one lens (the historic Tri-Elmar).  
Classic Line| Modern lenses that recreate or reinterpret historic designs.  
Nicknames| Collectors use shorthand: Lux, Cron and Noct; FLE for the floating element Summilux 35 of 2010; Steel Rim for a reissue of an early Summilux 35.
