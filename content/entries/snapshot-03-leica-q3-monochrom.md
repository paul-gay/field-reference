## The Story
**The Monochrom idea.** In 2012 Leica made a digital camera that could only shoot black and white. It became a line of its own.

**Into the Q.** The Q gained a Monochrom version with the Q2. The Q3 Monochrom followed in 2025.

**Why it matters.** Removing color isn't a limit. It's a choice that makes black and white better.

## The Craft
### Outer strengths
- All black, with no red dot: discreet on the street.
- The Q3 body, dials and macro ring.
- IP52 weather sealing.

### Inner strengths
- **No color filter** on the sensor: every pixel records pure luminance.
- **ISO up to 200,000** with fine, film like grain.
- **Content Credentials**, the first Q with them. Black and white video too.

## In Hand
### See
- Tonal depth on screen, from deep black to bright white.
- A portrait at high ISO in a dim corner.

### Touch
- The same Q controls as the color Q3.
- The macro ring.

### Feel
- The focus that comes from seeing only in black and white.
- Discretion: nothing on it announces itself.

> **Say during the demo**
>
> "This camera can't see color at all. Look what it does with light instead."

## In one frame
A Q that sees only light and shadow, and sees it better than any color camera converted to black and white.

## Choose it if
- You already see in black and white.
- Street photography and discretion matter.
- You shoot in low light.

## Ask
- "Do you picture your photos in color, or in black and white?"
- Portraits: consider the Q3 43.

## Pairs with
- A red or yellow filter (49mm) for skies
- Handgrip, spare battery
