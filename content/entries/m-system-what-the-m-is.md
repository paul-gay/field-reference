The M is Leica's interchangeable lens camera system built around the M bayonet mount, introduced with the M3 in 1954 and still in use today. Most M cameras are rangefinders: you focus by hand, looking through an optical window rather than through the lens. The system includes current digital bodies, film bodies Leica still manufactures, the M EV1 with an electronic viewfinder, and the most celebrated lens catalog in photography.

The M is small, quiet, and mechanical in feel. It asks the photographer to make decisions: focus, framing, often exposure. That's the point. It's a camera for people who want to be part of the process, not supervise it.

> **The watch analogy**
>
> An M is to other cameras what a hand wound manual watch is to a smartwatch. Nobody buys one because it's the easiest way to do the job. They buy it because doing the job with it is a pleasure, and because it will still be working, and serviceable, decades from now.
