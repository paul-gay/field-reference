## Care

  * Keep gear dry. Humidity invites fungus in lenses; store with silica gel or in a dry cabinet in humid climates like Houston.
  * Clean lenses with a blower first, then a soft brush, then a microfiber cloth or lens tissue with a little fluid.
  * Sensor dust shows at small apertures as dark spots. Use a blower with the camera facing down, or professional cleaning.
  * Remove batteries for long storage; keep spares charged.
  * Service mechanical film cameras periodically.

## Evaluating used equipment

Check| What to look for  
---|---  
Glass| Hold a flashlight behind the lens: haze, fungus (web like threads), separation, scratches. Light cleaning marks matter less than haze or fungus.  
Mechanics| Smooth focus, crisp aperture clicks, no loose parts.  
Rangefinder| Check alignment at infinity and close distances.  
Shutter| Test all speeds; shutter count for digital bodies where available.  
Body| Dents can hide deeper damage; check mount wear and screws.  
Provenance| Serial numbers, original box and papers; Leica Service Certificates add confidence.
