### Zone focusing

Instead of focusing each frame, preset the lens to a distance and aperture so a whole zone is acceptably sharp. Example: a 35mm lens at f/8 focused at 3 meters keeps roughly 2 to 7 meters in focus. Now you can shoot from the hip or at eye level without touching the focus ring. The depth of field scale on the lens shows the zone for each aperture.

### Hyperfocal focusing

For landscapes and wide scenes, set the infinity mark against your aperture on the depth of field scale. Everything from about half that distance to infinity will be sharp.

### Sunny 16

In bright sun, at f/16, set the shutter speed to 1 over your ISO (ISO 400 means 1/400, or the nearest, 1/500). Open one stop for hazy sun, two for overcast, three for open shade. Film shooters with an M-A live by this rule.

### Pre focusing and anticipation

Focus on a spot where something will happen, such as a doorway or a patch of light, then wait for the subject to enter. This is the classic street method and uses the M's view outside the frame.

### Holding steady

Rangefinders have no mirror slap and a gentle shutter, so you can often handhold slower speeds than with other cameras. A common starting guide is 1 over the focal length, then practice going slower.

### Choosing one lens

Many M photographers use one focal length for months. It trains the eye to see in that frame before raising the camera. 35mm and 50mm are the usual choices.
