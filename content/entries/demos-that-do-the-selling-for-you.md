## The rangefinder moment (M)

Hand them an M with a 35 or 50. "Look through the window. See the bright patch in the middle? It shows two images. Turn this ring until they become one." Wait for the reaction when the images snap together. Then point out the frame lines: "You can see outside the frame, so you watch people walk into your picture." Most people remember this demo for years.

## The one lens camera (Q3)

"It has one lens, a 28mm Summilux, and it's so sharp you can crop in the camera." Show the crop frames for longer focal lengths, then twist the macro ring and have them photograph something small, like the texture of a leather strap. Show the result on the screen. It turns "expensive camera" into "I could use this every day."

## The black and white only camera (Monochrom)

"This camera can only take black and white photos. We removed color from the sensor so every pixel sees pure light." Show a Monochrom image on screen, ideally a portrait. The idea of a camera that does less on purpose is the brand philosophy in one object.

## The souvenir (Sofort 2)

With the visitor's permission, take an instant photo of them in the store and hand it over. It's a gift they'll keep on the fridge, with the Leica name on the frame. Follow your store's policy on when to use this, since each print has a cost.

## The decoder (lenses)

For the curious: "The name tells you how much light it gathers. Noctilux is the night lens, Summilux is very fast, Summicron is f/2." People love being let in on a code.

## The weight and sound test

For anyone who says "it's just a camera": let them hold it, press the shutter, and turn the dials. Then hold a plastic camera for contrast, if the store keeps one. Say nothing. The hands understand quality before the mind does.
