If they love| Tell this| Then show  
---|---|---  
History| Barnack's hiking camera, the 1924 decision, the Leica I at the Leipzig Spring Fair in 1925, the M3 at Photokina in 1954.| An M, noting its mount still descends from 1954.  
Meaning and values| The Leica Freedom Train, where Ernst Leitz II helped Jewish employees and others leave Nazi Germany through Leitz offices abroad. Tell it with care and never as a sales line.| Nothing. Let the story sit.  
Famous photographs| Cartier-Bresson and "the decisive moment," Korda's portrait of Che Guevara, Eisenstaedt's V J Day kiss, the flag over the Reichstag.| The gallery wall, and a 50mm lens like Cartier-Bresson used.  
Design| The red dot, and the discreet versions without it, like the M11-P and SL3-P, chosen by photographers who don't want to be noticed.| An M11 and M11-P side by side.  
Craft| Around 45 M cameras a day in Wetzlar. Precision glass moulding for the new Noctilux-M 35.| A lens: the focus ring, the aperture clicks, the engraving.  
Technology| Content Credentials, which sign photos so edits can be detected, first built into the M11-P. Monochrom sensors that see only light.| A Monochrom file on screen.  
Value and collecting| Barnack's own prototype sold for €14.4 million in 2022, the world record for a camera. The first production M3 sold for €3.36 million in 2025.| Leica Pre-Owned and the trade in program.  
Watches| Ernst Leitz I trained in watchmaking in Neuchâtel. The ZM watches share the camera's instrument philosophy, and the CEO previously worked in a senior role at IWC.| A ZM 1 next to an M.  
Their phone| Leica's LUX app for iPhone and the Leica Leitzphone powered by Xiaomi bring Leica looks to phones.| Then a Q3, to show what a real sensor and lens add.  
  
> **Keep it true**
>
> Stick to facts you'd be comfortable seeing in print. Don't say a camera will increase in value, don't promise rumored products, and don't embellish famous photographer stories, since camera attributions are often disputed among enthusiasts.
